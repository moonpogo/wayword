# Trace Field Approved Language (v0)

Status: Canonical language bank for Trace Field v0 recurrence outputs.

## 1) Purpose

This file defines approved and prohibited language for Trace Field v0.

Core rule:
Wayword observes language behavior. It does not diagnose the user.

## 2) Approved Sentence Templates

Use these templates for user-facing recurrence statements:
- "This term appears across multiple runs."
- "This phrase recurs in separate entries."
- "These terms cluster in this period."
- "This image language returns over time."
- "These terms co-occur in repeated contexts."
- "This pattern appears again in later writing."
- "This recurrence is supported by the excerpts shown below."

Template rules:
- keep sentence length short
- state only observable recurrence
- avoid cause, motive, identity, or diagnosis claims

## 3) Approved Verbs

Allowed verbs:
- appears
- recurs
- clusters
- returns
- co-occurs (singular subject)
- co-occur (plural subject)

Also acceptable (limited use):
- repeats
- reappears
- surfaces (only for text pattern visibility, not identity revelation)

## 4) Prohibited Verbs

Do not use:
- reveals
- exposes
- indicates
- proves
- demonstrates psychologically
- diagnoses
- confirms
- predicts
- uncovers your true
- explains why you are

## 5) Approved Qualifiers

Use restrained qualifiers:
- "in this sample"
- "in these entries"
- "in this period"
- "appears to"
- "may reflect recurrence in wording"
- "based on visible text"

Qualifier rules:
- qualifiers should lower certainty, not create mystique
- if evidence is weak, suppress output rather than over-qualify

## 6) Prohibited Framing Examples

Do not use framing like:
- "your writing reveals who you are"
- "this indicates emotional instability"
- "this proves unresolved grief"
- "this exposes your attachment pattern"
- "you are avoiding intimacy"
- "optimize your writing performance with this insight"
- "keep your streak strong"

## 7) Approved Examples

- "Certain phrases recur across multiple runs."
- "Specific image language appears repeatedly."
- "A cluster of related terms returns in later entries."
- "These terms co-occur in several passages."

## 8) Borderline Examples (Rewrite Required)

Borderline:
- "This pattern indicates stress."
Rewrite:
- "This pattern recurs across these entries."

Borderline:
- "This reveals emotional weight."
Rewrite:
- "This image language appears repeatedly in this period."

Borderline:
- "You seem preoccupied with loss."
Rewrite:
- "Terms related to loss recur in multiple entries."

## 9) Prohibited Examples

- "Your writing suggests loneliness."
- "Themes of depression emerge."
- "You avoid intimacy."
- "Wayword understands what you feel."
- "This proves you are anxious."

## 10) Notes On Ambiguity Preservation

- Prefer description over interpretation.
- Do not collapse symbolic language into personal diagnosis.
- Keep outputs open enough for user ownership of meaning.
- If confidence is low, suppress the claim instead of stretching language.

## 11) Notes On Avoiding Faux Profundity

- Avoid mystical, ornamental, or prophetic tone.
- Avoid grand claims about truth, essence, or destiny.
- Use plain language anchored to visible evidence.
- Let restraint carry meaning.
