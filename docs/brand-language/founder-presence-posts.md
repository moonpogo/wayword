# Founder Presence Posts

Inputs:
- `docs/brand-language/wayword-language-base.md`

Purpose: define a human writing system for founder-facing social presence that stays alive, imperfect, and doctrine-aligned without becoming doctrine theater.

## 1) Founder Presence Philosophy

### Four language layers

- Doctrine language: constraint language. It defines boundaries and guardrails. It is stable, sparse, and durable.
- Product language: explanatory language. It helps people understand what the software does.
- Observatory language: system-language for time, recurrence, traces, silence, and return.
- Founder-presence language: lived weather. It is the human layer moving through all of the above in real time.

### Core clarification

The doctrine is the substrate.  
Founder voice is the human weather moving through it.

### Why all-posts-as-doctrine becomes dead

- It removes discovery.
- It sounds pre-decided.
- It flattens emotion into policy.
- It reads like a style guide talking to itself.

### Why roughness matters

- Roughness signals presence.
- Slightly uneven lines feel written by a person, not assembled by a system.
- Small contradictions feel credible when the work is still unfolding.

### Why lived specificity matters

- "I spent 40 minutes adjusting one line break in the reflection card" carries more trust than abstract principle language.
- Specific moments make doctrine believable.

### Why partiality matters

- Not every post should conclude.
- Unresolved observations mirror the product posture around weak signals.

### Why emotional grounding matters

- Emotional grounding is not confession theater.
- It is naming pressure, uncertainty, relief, irritation, and return in real proportions.

## 2) Human Post Archetypes

### Small realization
- Tone: light, observational, slightly surprised.
- Cadence: short sentence + one specific follow-up.
- Ideal length: 1-3 lines.
- Danger zones: turning realization into universal advice.
- Natural example shape: "I kept reaching for stronger language in the mirror cards, then realized quieter lines were more honest about the signal."

### Frustration while building
- Tone: candid, unglamorous, grounded.
- Cadence: concrete friction first, meaning second.
- Ideal length: 2-5 lines.
- Danger zones: melodrama, blaming users, shipping panic.
- Example shape: "Today was mostly me trying to stop the patterns surface from feeling like a dashboard. I removed labels more than I added anything."

### Noticing own behavior
- Tone: self-observational, non-performative.
- Cadence: "I noticed..." then one implication.
- Ideal length: 1-4 lines.
- Danger zones: self-branding as insight machine.
- Example shape: "I noticed I only trust reflective language when it names what is visible on the page."

### Writing confession
- Tone: honest, small-scale, non-grandiose.
- Cadence: admission + one concrete scene.
- Ideal length: 2-5 lines.
- Danger zones: therapy posting, identity claims.
- Example shape: "I still avoid submitting short drafts because low-signal states make me feel exposed."

### Interface observation
- Tone: precise, almost field-note.
- Cadence: visual detail -> user-feeling hypothesis.
- Ideal length: 2-4 lines.
- Danger zones: over-technical thread mode.
- Example shape: "When the reflection line sits too close to the editor, it feels like judgment. Two pixels changed the mood."

### Software culture irritation
- Tone: restrained irritation, no rant theater.
- Cadence: irritation + specific alternative.
- Ideal length: 1-3 lines.
- Danger zones: dunking for engagement.
- Example shape: "I keep seeing writing tools promise output. I care more about whether return feels possible."

### Process fragment
- Tone: notebook-like.
- Cadence: sentence fragment + unfinished thought.
- Ideal length: 1-3 lines.
- Danger zones: meaningless vagueness.
- Example shape: "Trying to keep signal language clipped. Still too eager to explain."

### Observatory discovery
- Tone: curious, precise.
- Cadence: what appeared + why it surprised you.
- Ideal length: 2-4 lines.
- Danger zones: fake certainty from thin data.
- Example shape: "Three saved runs were enough for a tiny recurrence cluster. Small, but it felt structurally real."

### Moment of uncertainty
- Tone: open, not helpless.
- Cadence: question + tentative direction.
- Ideal length: 1-4 lines.
- Danger zones: fishing for reassurance.
- Example shape: "Not sure yet if 'quiet evidence of return' is still alive language or if I’ve overused it."

### Visual artifact reflection
- Tone: archival, tactile.
- Cadence: describe artifact first, interpretation second.
- Ideal length: 2-4 lines.
- Danger zones: decorative mysticism.
- Example shape: "This season wheel is mostly empty space. It still feels like presence."

### Anecdotal memory
- Tone: grounded memory, low-drama.
- Cadence: memory detail + present consequence.
- Ideal length: 2-5 lines.
- Danger zones: nostalgia performance.
- Example shape: "A teacher once circled a sentence and wrote 'too certain.' I think about that every time I edit reflection copy."

### Encountering another writer/problem/tool
- Tone: respectful, practical.
- Cadence: encounter detail + what shifted.
- Ideal length: 2-5 lines.
- Danger zones: superiority posture.
- Example shape: "Someone told me they wanted mirror output to feel less smart. That line changed a week of copy decisions."

## 4) Doctrine Integration Rules

### Principle

Doctrine should emerge from thought, not arrive as slogan injection.

### Bad pattern

- Drop canonical phrase as a standalone stamp with no lived context.
- Example failure mode: posting "Observation over diagnosis." repeatedly without story or artifact.

### Better pattern

- Start from moment, friction, or observation.
- Let doctrine appear as conclusion, not headline.

### Example conversion

- Flat: "Observation over diagnosis."
- Human: "I kept realizing most writing software wanted to evaluate me somehow. Observation started feeling more honest than improvement language."

### Operating rules

- One doctrine anchor max per short post.
- Pair doctrine with one concrete detail.
- Avoid back-to-back posts that are pure doctrine lines.
- If no real moment exists, post artifact-only or stay silent.

## 5) Cadence and Texture Rules

### Signals of human, lived-in writing

- Uneven sentence lengths.
- Abrupt but natural transitions.
- Concrete nouns and moments.
- Occasional self-questioning.
- Admitted uncertainty.
- Partial endings.

### Signals of AI/polished/startup performance voice

- Perfectly symmetrical lines.
- Universal claims in every post.
- "Lesson learned" endings.
- Always-resolved threads.
- High-density abstract nouns with no scene detail.

### Cadence guidelines

- Mix one short declarative with one longer reflective sentence.
- Keep transitions light. "I noticed", "maybe", "still", "today".
- Allow one rough edge per post: an unfinished thought, a hedge, a question.
- Prefer single-post field notes over serial mini-threads.
- Keep rhetorical stakes local unless evidence justifies larger claims.

### Anti-thread cadence

- Avoid building every post as a 5-part argument.
- Avoid numbered wisdom sequences.
- Keep accumulation across weeks, not inside one post.

## 6) Vulnerability Boundaries

### Stay human without monetizing pain

- Share process pressure, not private crisis.
- Share uncertainty, not unresolved trauma.
- Share emotional texture tied to the work, not intimacy bait.

### Red lines

- No trauma dumping for engagement.
- No parasocial hooks.
- No therapy framing of users or self.
- No self-branding through exhaustion.

### Healthy vulnerability patterns

- "I was wrong about this copy decision."
- "I felt uncertain and paused the rollout."
- "This line felt too certain, so I cut it."

### Unhealthy patterns

- "I’m broken and this product saved me."
- "Sharing this because vulnerability performs."
- "My pain is the brand narrative."

Why uncertainty and roughness preserve authenticity:

- They reflect real-time thought formation.
- They prevent doctrine from becoming performance copy.
- They keep the account in observation mode rather than authority mode.

## 8) Posting Rhythm Philosophy

### Desired long-horizon feel

- Sparse accumulation.
- Recurring motifs.
- Occasional silence.
- Atmospheric continuity.
- Artifacts mixed with notes.
- Rough fragments beside polished essays.

### Rhythm guidelines

- Do not force daily posting.
- Let shipping cadence and observation cadence interact naturally.
- Post when there is an actual moment, artifact, or shift.
- Allow quiet periods without compensating with performative depth.

### Account texture over time

- Week to week should feel like field notes from an evolving system.
- Month to month should show recurring vocabulary without slogan repetition.

## 9) Anti-Patterns

### Startup founder voice
- Pattern: "building the future of writing."
- Correction: anchor to present-tense concrete work.

### AI-polished cadence
- Pattern: spotless symmetry and frictionless transitions.
- Correction: keep unevenness, local detail, and uncertainty.

### "In a world where..." language
- Pattern: staged macro framing.
- Correction: begin with direct observation.

### TED-talk language
- Pattern: over-resolved moral arc.
- Correction: leave room for open questions.

### Fake vulnerability
- Pattern: emotional reveal engineered for applause.
- Correction: share bounded, work-relevant uncertainty.

### Creator economy posting
- Pattern: audience growth framing, content pipeline talk.
- Correction: return to writing, interface, and observatory specifics.

### Engagement bait
- Pattern: hot takes and rhetorical traps.
- Correction: specific notes, calm tone, no CTA endings.

### Faux wisdom compression
- Pattern: every post as compressed doctrine.
- Correction: interleave scene-level observations and artifacts.

### Endless doctrine slogans
- Pattern: repeated canonical lines detached from lived context.
- Correction: doctrine appears as consequence of thought, not template.

## 10) Final Principle

Wayword’s public-facing founder voice should feel like a thoughtful human being trying to understand writing, software, atmosphere, and attention in real time.

It should read like someone present with the work, sometimes certain, sometimes unsure, often specific, occasionally unfinished.

The goal is recognizable human authorship over brand-performance intelligence theater.
