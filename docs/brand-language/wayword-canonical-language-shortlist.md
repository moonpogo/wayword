## 1) Canonical Core Phrases

### 1. `P001` "Wayword is an encounter instrument."
- Why it survives: distinctive thesis line with high conceptual compression.
- Emotional tone: precise, severe, intimate.
- Doctrine function: names product class without coach/productivity framing.
- Ideal usage: manifesto lead, product about, essay openers, deck thesis slide.
- Overuse risk: turns into brand costume if used as decorative caption.
- What weakens it: adding explanatory filler after it.

### 2. `P002` "Recognition over help."
- Why it survives: foundational decision rule in 3 words.
- Emotional tone: restrained conviction.
- Doctrine function: prevents advice voice drift.
- Ideal usage: doctrine docs, internal copy review checks, occasional social fragment.

## 4) Canonical Cadence Analysis

- Authored rhythm: short declaratives and clipped two-part statements dominate.
- Asymmetry: strongest lines often have unequal clause lengths (`signal is thin` + `add surface`).
- Compression: doctrinal lines compress policy into 3-6 words (`recognition over help`, `observation over diagnosis`).
- Fragment handling: atmospheric lines stay intentionally incomplete (`temporal mirror`, `quiet evidence of return`).
- Restraint mechanism: verbs describe visible behavior, rarely intent.
- Verb set center: observe, return, surface, drift, compress, open.
- Scientific-literary balance: technical nouns (`signal`, `pattern`, `surface`) are paired with temporal-atmospheric nouns (`season`, `silence`, `return`).
- Anti-slogan behavior: avoids symmetry chains and motivational closure.
- Anti-AI cadence behavior: avoids universal claims, avoids "unlock/transform/elevate" verbs.

What makes it feel authored:
- uneven cadence
- high specificity nouns
- bounded claims
- operational humility

What makes it stop feeling authored:
- stacked parallel aphorisms
- TED-talk cadence
- explanatory padding
- polished certainty
- engagement-oriented CTA tails

## 5) Irreplaceable Vocabulary

### observatory
- Wayword meaning: longitudinal perceptual surface, not analytics panel.
- Emotional coloration: contemplative rigor.
- Visual implication: instruments, fields, seasonal maps.
- Overuse risk: worldbuilding jargon if used without artifact context.

### recurrence
- Meaning: repeated language return across runs.
- Coloration: patient recognition.
- Visual implication: cluster echoes, trace density.
- Overuse risk: abstraction drift without examples.

### residue
- Meaning: linguistic remainder that persists across drafts.
- Coloration: material, tactile.
- Visual implication: strata, sediment, trace marks.
- Overuse risk: faux literary fog if detached from text evidence.

### surface
- Meaning: visible text behavior on the page.
- Coloration: concrete and procedural.
- Visual implication: editor crop, card-level reflection.
- Overuse risk: flattening into generic UI term.

### drift
- Meaning: directional change across time or within draft.
- Coloration: subtle movement.
- Visual implication: vector, flow, seasonal shift maps.
- Overuse risk: vague metaphor if not tied to observable shifts.

### cadence
- Meaning: sentence-length and pressure behavior.
- Coloration: analytic, embodied.
- Visual implication: pulse traces, line-length charts.
- Overuse risk: over-technical tone.

### return
- Meaning: repeated reappearance of language/time presence.
- Coloration: quiet continuity.
- Visual implication: cyclical artifacts, recurring marks.
- Overuse risk: sentimentality.

### restraint
- Meaning: bounded interpretation and claim discipline.
- Coloration: ethical calm.
- Visual implication: sparse states, suppression transparency.
- Overuse risk: moral posture without evidence.

### field
- Meaning: distributed pattern space rather than score panel.
- Coloration: infrastructural openness.
- Visual implication: non-grid cluster maps.
- Overuse risk: conceptual vagueness.

### traces
- Meaning: retained writing signals over time.
- Coloration: archival and forensic.
- Visual implication: artifacts, layered marks, low-opacity recurrence.
- Overuse risk: becoming decorative language token.

## 6) Red Flag Drift Signals

### Startup collapse
- Warning examples: "platform", "unlock", "scale your writing", "insight engine."
- Correction pattern: return to bounded observable claim (`reflects visible patterns`, `signal is thin`).

### Creator-economy contamination
- Warning examples: "build your audience voice", "content velocity", "creator workflow."
- Correction pattern: restore writing-surface and observatory vocabulary.

### Productivity contamination
- Warning examples: "streak", "output goals", "performance tracking."
- Correction pattern: season/return language and anti-dashboard constraints.

### Therapy contamination
- Warning examples: "healing journey", "emotional diagnosis", "what you are avoiding."
- Correction pattern: observation verbs and evidence-bounded phrasing.

### AI-tool contamination
- Warning examples: "AI writing copilot", "smart rewrite assistant", "personalized generative coach."
- Correction pattern: encounter instrument + reflection without diagnosis.

### Decorative mysticism
- Warning examples: "sacred essence", "hidden truth beneath language", "destiny of the draft."
- Correction pattern: concrete behavior readouts and suppression humility.

### Over-explanation
- Warning examples: multi-clause doctrine paragraphs where one line works.
- Correction pattern: shorten to a single bounded sentence plus one concrete example.

### TED-talk cadence
- Warning examples: "we believe that...", "in a world where...", "the future of writing is..."
- Correction pattern: clipped operational statements.

### Symmetrical aphorism disease
- Warning examples: serial "X over Y" chains in one block.
- Correction pattern: mix with uneven observational lines and fragments.
